# I_AM_A_GOOD_TRADER.md — Colin's trading doctrine, in his own words
Rule #1 of this repo. Dictated 2026-08-03, first live morning. This is the discretionary layer — the part no model replaces. Saved verbatim in structure, cleaned only for reading.

## The regime triad
The market is always doing exactly one of three things: **consolidating, manipulating, or continuing.** Call it market regime. When it's moving you can only bet on one of the three.

## The clock (NY time — watched happen every morning since I was a young trader)
- **9:30–10:30** — the day's first real move. Big rally or dump, this is where the day shows its hand.
- **10:30 — hard shift.** Now it's manipulation, or range/support-resistance chop, or it keeps ripping on news, or anywhere in between. The read changes at 10:30, every day.

## The two-bet sequence
**Bet 1 (small, 1–5 min timeframe):** bet the move continues a little further. The crowd then decides — reverse, or blast through your TP or SL. 50/50. If TP hits: you took your shot, you made your $300, you live to fight another day. Done.

**Bet 2 (SMALLER than bet 1, wider room):** only if bet 1 loses. Smaller size, more room, because the assumption is price retraced into the FVG. Plays out over time — this is the patient leg, not the revenge leg. Size goes DOWN after a loss, never up. Room goes up, size goes down.

## TP/SL doctrine
Price is sloppy. **Too tight with TP and SL = being early = being WRONG.** Place them lenient. That's what discretionary means.

Trailing TP and SL, very dynamic, very Stockfish. Securing wins is rule #1: do not lose.

## The exit doctrine (the core of everything)
**It doesn't matter the entry. It matters all about the exit.** If the entry is wrong but the exit is perfect every time, you still end up a profitable trader. Break-even on a bad day is a win — "the loss retraced and we said nope, it only gets worse from here. 1 and done."

## The daily blueprint
Know what you want out of the market every morning BEFORE it opens. That is the blueprint.

- Enough capital + RR = set goal (for the funded eval: **$300/day**)
- Small capital + bigger RR = TP1: don't lose on the day (break even) · TP2: make the set goal ($300) · TP3: trail anything extra — you never know when it rips, the market and everyone in it are unpredictable.

Acceptable exits for the day, all of them wins: +$300 (the eval target), +$1000 (it ripped), $0 (breakeven, protected), never a full loss taken hoping.

## The edge
On NASDAQ we don't care what the company is. We care about its **news** — what its shareholders are thinking — and how we exit the day with more money than we started. The edge is the research you do every morning: what do the shareholders think today. Everything else is drawings on a chart and the power of %.
